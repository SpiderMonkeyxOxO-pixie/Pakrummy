## Direct answer

Pak Rummy's core game, 13-card Rummy, is won by arranging all 13 cards into
valid sequences and sets — with at least one pure sequence — before any
opponent does. This page is the full rulebook: deal structure, what counts as
a valid group, every valid declaration combination, and the specific rule
violations that make a declaration invalid.

## Core rules at a glance

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Cards dealt | 13 per player |
| Deck | Two standard decks shuffled together, plus printed jokers |
| Players | Typically 2 to 6 |
| Minimum pure sequences | 1 (no joker used) |
| Minimum sequences overall | 2 |
| Turn action | Draw 1 card, discard 1 card |
| Winning condition | Valid declaration with all 13 cards grouped |

## Deal and turn structure

Each player is dealt 13 cards. One card is turned face-up to start the
discard pile, and a separate card is designated the wild joker for that
round — any card of that rank, in any suit, can stand in for a missing card
in a set or sequence. On your turn you draw one card, from either the closed
deck or the discard pile, then discard one card face-up. Play continues
clockwise until someone declares.

## Sequences vs. sets

A **sequence** is three or more consecutive cards of the same suit. A **pure
sequence** is a sequence built with no joker at all — at least one of these
is mandatory in every valid hand. A **set** is three or more cards of the
same rank in different suits, and a joker can fill a set's missing card.

## The valid declaration combinations

All 13 cards must resolve into one of these three shapes:

- 2 sequences + 2 sets
- 3 sequences + 1 set
- 4 sequences

In every combination, at least two groups must be sequences, and at least
one of those must be pure. There's no version of the rules where a four-set
hand is valid, no matter how neatly the cards fit — the pure sequence
requirement doesn't bend for a clean-looking arrangement.

## What makes a declaration invalid

These are the specific rule violations that void a declare, not general play
advice:

- **No pure sequence anywhere in the hand.** Four complete sets, or three
  sequences built entirely with jokers, still fail — a pure sequence is
  non-negotiable.
- **Fewer than two sequences.** A hand of mostly sets with only one sequence
  (pure or not) doesn't meet the minimum.
- **A group that looks complete but isn't.** A "sequence" with a gap (e.g.,
  4♠-6♠-7♠ missing the 5♠, and no joker covering it) isn't a valid group —
  it's just three unconnected cards.
- **Declaring with the wrong card count.** All 13 cards must be accounted
  for across your groups; any card left ungrouped blocks the declaration.

An invalid declaration is scored as if the player held no valid groups at
all — see our [scoring guide](/guides/rummy-scoring-explained/) for exactly
how that penalty is calculated.

## Formats: how these rules get scored

The same rules above apply across Pak Rummy's standard formats — Points
Rummy (one round at a time), Pool Rummy (cumulative score with elimination),
and Deals Rummy (a fixed number of rounds). The rules for forming a valid
hand don't change between formats; only how the resulting points convert to
a result does. Full breakdown, including the card-value table and a worked
scoring example, is in our

## FAQ

**What is a pure sequence in Pak Rummy?**
Three or more consecutive cards of the same suit, with no joker used
anywhere in that group. Every valid hand needs at least one.

**Can I win without a pure sequence?**
No. Regardless of how the rest of your 13 cards are grouped, a declaration
without at least one pure sequence is invalid.

**How many cards are dealt in Pak Rummy?**
13 cards per player, from two standard decks shuffled together plus printed
jokers.

**What happens if my declaration is ruled invalid?**
It's scored as if you had no valid groups — see our
[scoring guide](/guides/rummy-scoring-explained/) for the exact point
calculation.

**What's the difference between a sequence and a set?**
A sequence is three or more consecutive cards of one suit. A set is three or
more cards of the same rank across different suits.
