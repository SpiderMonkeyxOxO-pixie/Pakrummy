## Direct answer

In 13-card Rummy, every unmatched card left in your hand at the end of a round
("deadwood") counts against you: face cards and Aces are worth 10 points each,
number cards are worth their face value, and a valid declaration with zero
deadwood scores 0. The three common formats — Points, Pool, and Deals — differ
only in how those per-round points convert into money or elimination.

## Card values

| Card | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Ace, King, Queen, Jack | 10 |
| Number cards (2–10) | Face value |
| Joker (printed or wild) | 0 |

## Points Rummy

Each round is scored independently. The winner scores 0; every other player's
deadwood is totalled (usually capped at a maximum, commonly 80 points) and
multiplied by an agreed point value to produce a result for that single round.

## Pool Rummy

Players accumulate deadwood points across multiple rounds instead of one.
Common formats are 101-point pool and 201-point pool. A player is eliminated
once their cumulative score crosses the pool limit; the game continues until
one player remains.

## Deals Rummy

A fixed number of rounds ("deals") are agreed before play starts — commonly 2,
3, or 6. Each player starts every deal with equal chips. At the end of a round,
chips move from losing players to the winner based on deadwood, and the
player with the most chips after the final deal wins overall.

## Worked example

Suppose a player declares with these unmatched cards left in hand: K♠, 9♦, 4♣.
That's 10 (King) + 9 + 4 = 23 deadwood points for that round. In Points Rummy
at ₨1 per point, that round would cost the player ₨23. In Pool Rummy, those 23
points are added to their running total toward the elimination threshold.
