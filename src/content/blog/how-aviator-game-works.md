## Direct answer

The Aviator game is a multiplier-based betting game: a plane takes off and a multiplier rises in real time, and players must cash out before the round ends or lose their stake. For example, a 100 PKR bet cashed out at 2.0x returns 200 PKR; waiting too long forfeits the bet entirely.

## Why this page reads differently from most Aviator content

Search results for Aviator are crowded with pages promising "easy winnings," "guaranteed signals," or "the fastest withdrawals." This page does the opposite: it explains the mechanic as plainly as possible, including the part hype pages leave out — you can lose your whole stake on any round, and nothing you can see in advance changes that.

## How a round works, step by step

1. **You place a bet** before the round starts, choosing a stake.
2. **The plane takes off** and a multiplier starts at 1.00x and climbs in real time.
3. **You decide when to cash out.** Pressing cash-out locks in your stake multiplied by whatever the multiplier shows at that moment.
4. **The round ends at an unpredictable point.** At some moment the plane "flies away" and the multiplier stops. Anyone who hasn't cashed out by then loses the stake for that round.

The only decision you make is *when* to cash out. You don't influence where the round ends.

## A worked example

This is an illustration of the arithmetic, not a promise of any result.

- You bet **100 PKR**.
- You cash out when the multiplier shows **2.0x**. You receive 100 × 2.0 = **200 PKR**.
- If instead the plane flies away at 1.6x while you are still waiting for 2.0x, the round ends and the **100 PKR stake is lost**.

A higher target multiplier means a larger possible payout, but it also means the round has to last longer for you to reach it. Neither outcome is guaranteed.

## Why the crash point can't be predicted

In crash-style games generally, the point where a round ends is produced by an algorithm, not by a visible pattern. Each round is independent: what happened in the previous ten rounds does not change what happens in the next one. That is why claims such as "a big multiplier is due" or "this signal predicts the next crash" are false — they treat independent rounds as if they were connected, which is the gambler's fallacy.

Many crash games describe their fairness using cryptographic or "provably fair" methods, where the outcome is fixed before the round begins rather than adjusted live. That describes the general approach in the genre. We have not independently verified how the Aviator title in the Pak Rummy app generates its rounds, so we are not making that claim about this specific app.

## Risk framing

This is not a strategy guide, and there is no "tips to win" section, because no tip can change the odds of a round you cannot predict. If you play, decide your budget and your cash-out target before the round starts rather than while the multiplier is climbing, and stop when the budget is gone. A fast-moving game makes it easy to play longer or stake more than you planned.

## FAQ

**Can you predict when it will crash?**
No. Each round's crash point is independent and can't be reliably predicted. Treat any "pattern," "signal," or "prediction" claim as false.

**What happens if I don't cash out in time?**
The round ends and the stake for that round is lost.

**Is Aviator the same as other crash games?**
Aviator is one specific branded title within the broader crash-game genre. The core mechanic — a rising multiplier and a cash-out decision — is shared; see [how crash games work](/blog/how-crash-games-work/) for the general explanation.
