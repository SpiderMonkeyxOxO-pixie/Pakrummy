## Direct answer

13-card Rummy requires arranging all 13 cards into valid sequences and sets — at least two sequences, with one being a pure sequence using no joker — before any opponent does. Players draw one card and discard one card each turn until someone declares a valid hand.

## Deal and turn structure

Each player is dealt 13 cards from two standard decks shuffled together, plus printed jokers. One card is turned face-up to start the discard pile, and a random card is also designated the **wild joker** for that round — any card of that rank, in any suit, can substitute for a missing card in a set or sequence. On your turn, you draw one card — either from the closed deck or the top of the discard pile — then discard one card face-up. Play continues clockwise until a player declares.

## What counts as a valid declaration

A valid declaration needs all 13 cards grouped into sequences and sets using one of these combinations:

- **2 sequences + 2 sets**, or
- **3 sequences + 1 set**, or
- **4 sequences**

Whichever combination you use, **at least two of your groups must be sequences, and at least one of those sequences must be pure** — three or more consecutive cards of the same suit, with no joker used. A set is three or more cards of the same rank in different suits. You cannot declare with a valid set-and-sequence count that skips the pure-sequence requirement, even if every card is technically placed somewhere — that hand is an invalid declaration and is scored as if you had no valid groups at all. See [how that scoring penalty actually works](/guides/rummy-scoring-explained/).

## Worked example: one full hand from deal to declare

Say you're dealt these 13 cards: 3♠ 4♠ 5♠ · 7♥ 8♥ 9♥ · 10♣ 10♦ 10♠ · K♣ K♥ · 2♦ · J♠, with the 6 of any suit set as the wild joker for this round.

Two groups are already sitting in your hand: **3♠-4♠-5♠** (a pure sequence — three consecutive spades, no joker) and **7♥-8♥-9♥** (a second pure sequence). **10♣-10♦-10♠** is a complete set. That's three valid groups using 9 of your 13 cards, with the pure-sequence requirement already satisfied twice over.

The remaining four cards — K♣, K♥, 2♦, and J♠ — don't connect to anything yet. Over the next few turns, you discard the 2♦ and J♠ since neither fits your existing groups or has a realistic path to one, and you draw toward completing K♣-K♥ into a set. When you draw a third King, or pick up a 6 (the wild joker) to substitute for one, K♣-K♥-[King or wild 6] becomes your fourth group.

Final declared hand: 3♠-4♠-5♠ (pure sequence) + 7♥-8♥-9♥ (pure sequence) + 10♣-10♦-10♠ (set) + K♣-K♥-6♦(wild) (set). That's 2 sequences + 2 sets, with two pure sequences — a clean, valid declaration.

## How scoring works once someone declares

When a player declares, every other player's unmatched cards — their "deadwood" — are totaled using each card's face value (number cards at face value, face cards and Aces typically worth 10 points, jokers worth 0). The player with the lowest deadwood total loses the least; the declaring player, if their declaration is valid, usually scores zero for the round. The exact point thresholds and format (Points, Pool, or Deals) vary by table — see our [full scoring breakdown](/guides/rummy-scoring-explained/) for the math.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Discarding a card you'll need later.** Sort your hand by suit as soon as you pick it up, and hold onto cards with a realistic path to a sequence longer than you'd think.
- **Forgetting the pure-sequence requirement.** A hand with four sets and zero pure sequences cannot be declared, no matter how complete it looks.
- **Misreading the wild joker.** The wild joker changes every round — a card that substituted for anything last round may just be an ordinary card this round.

## FAQ

**What's the difference between a sequence and a set?**
A sequence is three or more consecutive cards of the same suit. A set is three or more cards of the same rank, in different suits.

**Can I declare without a pure sequence?**
No. At least one pure sequence — no joker used — is required regardless of how your other cards are grouped.

**How many decks are used?**
Two standard decks shuffled together, plus printed jokers, typically for 2 to 6 players.
