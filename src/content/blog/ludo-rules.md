## Direct answer

Ludo is a race game for 2 to 4 players: each player moves four tokens around a cross-shaped board toward the center, needing a roll of 6 to leave base. The first player to bring all four tokens into the center triangle, by exact roll, wins.

## Board and setup

The Ludo board is a 15×15 grid shaped like a cross, with four colored home bases — red, blue, green, and yellow — in the corners, and a shared 52-square track looping around the outside. In the middle sits the home triangle, the finishing point for every token. Each player starts with all four of their tokens inside their own colored base.

## Rolling and moving

Play moves clockwise. On each turn, a player rolls a single six-sided die:

- A token can only **leave the base** on a roll of 6, moving onto its colored starting square.
- You move **one token per roll**, forward exactly the number of pips shown.
- Rolling a **6 grants another roll** — but in most house rules, rolling three 6s in a row forfeits the turn instead of granting a fourth.

## Capturing opponents' tokens

If your token lands on a square occupied by an opponent's token, that opponent's token is sent back to their base — they'll need to roll a 6 again before it can re-enter play. Some squares are marked safe (usually the starting squares and a few marked positions around the track), where captures can't happen.

## Winning the game

The first player to move all four of their tokens all the way around the track and into the center home triangle wins. A token must reach the center by an **exact roll** — if the roll would overshoot, the move isn't allowed and that token stays where it is until a smaller roll lets it finish.

## How digital Ludo often differs from the board game

If you're opening a Ludo table inside a real-money gaming app for the first time, expect some differences from the physical board game described above — these are common app conventions, not universal rules:

- **Auto-roll or tap-to-roll** interfaces replace a physical die.
- **Forced-move settings** — some apps require you to move a token if a legal move exists, rather than letting you skip a turn by choice.
- **Bonus squares** that award extra moves or small in-game bonuses, which don't exist in the traditional board game.

Exactly which of these apply on any specific table is configured by that app, not by the classic rules above — check the table's own info screen before you rely on an assumption.
