## Direct answer

Set a fixed amount you can afford to lose completely before you open the app — not after you've started playing — keep that amount separate from money earmarked for bills or essentials, and stop the moment you hit it. The habit matters more than the exact number: a budget only works if you decide it in advance and follow it regardless of how a session is going.

## Why this matters before you play

Pak Rummy is a real-money game for adults 18 and older. It's entertainment, not a source of income, and it carries real financial risk — that's the starting point on our [responsible gaming](/responsible-gaming/) page, and it's the same starting point here. A budget is simply the practical tool that turns "play responsibly" from a general intention into something you actually do before the first hand is dealt.

The reason a budget has to be set in advance, not adjusted mid-session, is straightforward: once you're playing, especially after a loss, your judgment about what's "reasonable" shifts. Deciding your limit while you're calm and not in the middle of a game removes that problem entirely.

## How to set your budget

1. **Pick a fixed loss limit, not a win target.** Decide the maximum amount you're willing to lose in a single session and in a given week — and treat that number as the real constraint. A "goal" of how much you want to win doesn't protect you the same way, because it doesn't stop you from playing longer after a loss.
2. **Base it on money you can afford to lose completely.** This should be discretionary spending, the same category you'd put toward any other entertainment — not rent, bills, savings, or borrowed money. If losing the full amount would cause you financial stress, the number is too high.
3. **Separate gaming funds from essential funds.** Keep the money you've set aside for play in a distinct wallet balance or account, apart from the funds you rely on for everyday expenses. This isn't about the app enforcing anything — it's a manual habit that makes it much harder to accidentally overspend, because you're never tempted to "just top up from the main account this once."
4. **Set a weekly or monthly ceiling, not just a per-session one.** A session limit stops a single bad night from spiraling; a weekly or monthly ceiling stops several smaller sessions from quietly adding up to more than you intended.
5. **Decide your limit before you open the app**, every time — not as a number you keep in your head loosely, but as something you commit to before you start, the same way you'd set a budget before walking into a shop.

## Tracking deposits vs. withdrawals

A budget is only useful if you can see whether you're keeping to it. A simple way to do that without needing any special tool:

- Keep a running note (a notes app, a small notebook, or a simple spreadsheet) of every deposit you make and the date.
- Log withdrawals the same way.
- Compare the two periodically — weekly is a reasonable cadence — so you have an honest, dated record of net spending rather than a rough impression. It's easy to underestimate how much has gone in over several smaller deposits; a written log corrects for that.

This also gives you an objective point of reference: if your log shows you've already deposited your full weekly budget, that's your stop signal, regardless of how the next session feels like it might go.

## Warning signs it's time to stop

Stop for the day — and reconsider your budget itself — if you notice any of the following:

- You're increasing your stakes or session length specifically to "win back" a loss. This is one of the clearest signs of harmful play, not a normal recovery strategy.
- You've gone past the limit you set for yourself and are telling yourself "just this once."
- You're playing to escape stress, sadness, or boredom rather than for enjoyment.
- You're not being fully honest with family or friends about how much time or money you're spending.
- You feel irritable or anxious at the thought of cutting back or stopping.
- You've considered borrowing money, from a person, a loan app, or a credit line, to keep playing.

If any of these sound familiar, see the full list on our [responsible gaming](/responsible-gaming/) page, and consider speaking to a trusted family member, a licensed counsellor, or a national mental-health helpline in Pakistan — this is more effective and more immediate than anything an app or website can offer.

## FAQ

**How much should my gaming budget be?**
There's no universal number — it should be an amount you can afford to lose completely without it affecting your bills, savings, or essential spending. If you're unsure, start smaller than feels necessary; you can always keep the same limit next week, but recovering from overspending is harder.

**Should I set a win target instead of a loss limit?**
No — a loss limit is the one that actually protects you. A win target doesn't stop you from continuing to play after a loss, which is when budgets tend to break down.

**Does Pak Rummy have a built-in deposit limit or self-exclusion feature?**
We haven't confirmed this yet. If the app offers these inside account settings, use them — but don't wait to find out; set your own manual limit regardless, as described above.

**What should I do if I've already gone over budget?**
Stop the session. Don't try to make it back by playing more — that's the single most common path from a manageable loss to a harmful one. Review your deposit/withdrawal log, reset your limit for next time if needed, and see our [responsible gaming](/responsible-gaming/) page for further guidance.
