## Direct answer

A rejected identity verification almost always comes down to one of four things: the ID photo itself (blurry, cropped, glare, or an expired document), a name that doesn't match exactly between your ID and your payment method, a selfie or liveness check taken in poor conditions, or a file that wasn't a direct photo at all — a screenshot or a photo of a screen instead of the physical document. None of these mean your account is blocked or under review for a problem with you; they're the same handful of reasons identity checks get rejected across real-money apps generally, and each one has a specific fix.

## Why this happens

Identity verification on real-money gaming apps works by matching a photo of your government-issued ID against a selfie or short liveness check, then checking that the name on both matches the payment method you use. That matching step is mostly automated, which means it's also unforgiving about image quality — a check that a human would pass on sight (a slightly blurry photo, a name with a middle initial added) can fail automated matching even though nothing is actually wrong with your identity or your account. Our [account verification guide](/account-verification/) covers what's typically requested; this page goes deeper into why a submission specifically gets rejected and what to do about each cause.

## The most likely causes, checked in order

### 1. The ID photo itself didn't pass

This is the single most common cause. Glare across the surface of the card, a corner or edge cut off in the frame, text that's too small or blurry to read, or a document that's expired all cause an automated check to fail even when the ID is completely genuine. Retake the photo in even daylight (not direct flash, which causes glare), lay the document flat, and make sure all four corners and every line of text are inside the frame and legible before you submit.

### 2. The name doesn't match exactly

Verification checks typically compare the name on your ID against the name registered to your payment method, and even a small mismatch — a missing middle name, a nickname instead of your legal name, or a different transliteration of the same name — can trigger a rejection. The fix is matching both to your ID exactly, not to whichever version of your name you're used to typing: if your payment method is registered under a different name, that's usually the thing to correct, not the ID submission.

### 3. The selfie or liveness check failed

A selfie taken with a beauty filter, heavy makeup that visibly differs from your ID photo, poor or uneven lighting, or sunglasses/a hat obscuring your face can all cause a liveness check to reject the match. Retake it with your face fully visible, in even lighting, with no filter applied, following whatever on-screen prompt the liveness step gives you (turning your head, blinking) exactly as instructed rather than rushing through it.

### 4. A screenshot or photo of a screen was submitted instead of the physical document

Some verification rejections happen because the uploaded file isn't a direct photo of the physical ID at all — it's a screenshot of a digital copy, or a photo taken of the document displayed on another screen. Both typically fail because of the added glare, pixelation, or screen-door moiré pattern that an automated check reads as tampering, even when the underlying document is genuine. Submit a direct photo of the physical card itself, taken with your phone's camera, not a copy of a copy.

### 5. The document type itself isn't one the app accepts

Apps in this category commonly accept one specific primary ID type — in Pakistan, typically a CNIC — rather than any government-issued document. If you submitted a different form of ID (a driving licence, a passport, a student card), it may be rejected outright regardless of image quality, simply because it isn't the document type the check is configured to accept. We haven't independently confirmed Pak Rummy's exact accepted-document list beyond what's stated on our [account verification page](/account-verification/); if your CNIC is also being rejected after fixing the issues above, that's a case for customer service rather than a document-type problem.

## If you've fixed all of the above and it's still rejected

- Wait for the current submission to finish processing before resubmitting — repeatedly resubmitting while a check is still pending can itself cause confusion about which attempt is the active one.
- Double-check your ID hasn't actually expired; an expired document is rejected regardless of photo quality.
- Try taking the photo on a different device if one is available, to rule out a camera or file-size issue specific to your phone.
- If it's still rejected after a clean resubmission, contact [customer service](/customer-service/) with your registered number and roughly when you submitted — they can tell you the specific rejection reason from their side, which isn't visible from the app alone.

## FAQ

**Does a rejected verification mean something is wrong with my identity or account?**
No. The vast majority of rejections are image-quality or name-matching issues with the submission itself, not a problem with the account or a sign you've been flagged for anything.

**Why would my ID be rejected if it's completely genuine?**
Automated matching checks are strict about image quality and exact name matching in ways a human reviewer wouldn't be — glare, a cropped corner, or a name variant can fail the check even though the document itself is entirely valid.

**I submitted a screenshot of my digital ID instead of a photo. Could that be why it failed?**
Yes, this is a common and often-overlooked cause. Submit a direct camera photo of the physical document rather than a screenshot or a photo of another screen.

**My name on my ID and my payment method genuinely don't match. What do I do?**
Update the name on whichever account is incorrect so both match your legal ID exactly, rather than resubmitting the same mismatched pair repeatedly — that mismatch won't resolve itself on a retry.

**How many times can I resubmit before I should just contact support?**
There's no number we can confirm for Pak Rummy specifically. If a clean resubmission (good lighting, uncropped, correct document type, matching name) still fails, that's the point to contact customer service rather than keep retrying the same fixes.
