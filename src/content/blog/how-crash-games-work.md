## Direct answer

A crash game is a betting format where a multiplier rises from 1.00x until an unpredictable "crash point," and players must cash out before that point to win; waiting too long loses the full stake. Each round is mathematically independent of the last.

## How the multiplier rises

Every round starts after bets are placed. A multiplier begins at 1.00x and climbs continuously, usually shown as a curve or a counter. At any moment before the round ends, you can cash out: your stake is multiplied by whatever the multiplier shows at that instant. If you cash out at 1.5x on a 100 PKR bet, you receive 150 PKR. Nothing is paid out automatically — the multiplier only converts to money when you actively cash out.

## What "crash point" means

The crash point is the multiplier value at which the round ends. If you haven't cashed out by then, the stake for that round is lost. The crash point can be low or high, and you don't see it coming. Reputable implementations typically use an algorithmic or cryptographic method that fixes the crash point before the round starts rather than adjusting it while you watch.

## Why every round is independent

Each round is generated separately from the previous one. A run of early crashes does not make a big multiplier "due," and a run of high multipliers does not make a crash "due" either. Believing otherwise is the gambler's fallacy, and it is exactly the idea that "pattern," "signal" and "prediction" content tries to exploit. No past sequence of rounds tells you what the next one will do.

## Crash games and Aviator

"Crash game" is the name of the genre; Aviator is one branded title within it. Aviator presents the rising multiplier as a plane taking off, but the underlying mechanic — rising multiplier, cash out before it ends — is shared. ## Risk framing

This isn't a strategy guide, and no cash-out habit guarantees an outcome — crash games are chance-based. If you choose to play, decide your budget and your cash-out target before the round starts, not while the multiplier is climbing. That is a budgeting habit, not a way to beat the game. Aviator is one branded example; "crash game" is the general genre name for the mechanic.

**Can the house control when it crashes?**
