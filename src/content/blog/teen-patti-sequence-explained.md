## Direct answer

Teen Patti hand sequence, highest to lowest: Trail (three of a kind), Pure Sequence (straight flush), Sequence (straight), Color (flush), Pair, and High Card. Ties within the same hand type are broken by comparing card rank, highest first.

## The full ranking table

| Rank | Hand | What it means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Trail** | Three cards of the same rank | 7-7-7 |
| 2 | **Pure Sequence** | Three consecutive ranks, all the same suit | 4-5-6, all hearts |
| 3 | **Sequence** | Three consecutive ranks, mixed suits | 4-5-6, mixed suits |
| 4 | **Color** | Three cards of the same suit, not consecutive | 2-7-J, all spades |
| 5 | **Pair** | Two cards of the same rank | 9-9-4 |
| 6 | **High Card** | None of the above | K-8-3, mixed suits |

A hand always counts as the highest type it qualifies for. A hand that is both consecutive and all one suit is a Pure Sequence, not a Sequence or a Color.

## Why the order runs this way

The ranking follows how rare each hand is in a 3-card deal. Three of a kind is the hardest to get, so it sits on top. A Pure Sequence needs both consecutive ranks and a single suit, so it is rarer than a plain Sequence or a plain Color. In a 3-card game, a Sequence is harder to make than a Color, which is why Teen Patti ranks them in that order. This differs from 5-card poker, where a flush beats a straight.

## Worked examples

- **Trail vs. Pure Sequence:** 3-3-3 beats Q-K-A of hearts. A Trail beats every other hand type.
- **Sequence vs. Color:** 5-6-7 in mixed suits beats A-9-4 all in diamonds. Hand type decides it, and card rank only matters within the same type.
- **Pair vs. High Card:** 4-4-2 beats A-K-J. Even the lowest pair beats the strongest High Card hand.
- **Same type, compare rank:** 9-9-2 beats 8-8-K. For two Pairs, compare the paired rank first, then the remaining card if the pairs match.

## How ties are broken

When two players hold the same hand type, compare card rank:

- **Two Trails:** the higher rank wins, so 9-9-9 beats 7-7-7.
- **Two Sequences or Pure Sequences:** compare the highest card in each sequence.
- **Two Colors or two High Cards:** compare the highest card first, then the second, then the third.
- **Two Pairs:** compare the paired rank, then the odd card.

Suit does not break ties. If two hands are identical in rank, the usual rule is that the pot is split, though how that is handled can vary by table.

## Ace high and Ace low

Ace ranks highest in a Trail, so A-A-A beats K-K-K. In a sequence, Ace can sit at the bottom as A-2-3. Whether Q-K-A counts as a sequence, and where A-2-3 ranks against other sequences, differs between house rules. Always check the ruleset of the table you are playing.

## FAQ

**Does suit matter when comparing two sequences?**
No. A Sequence beats a Color regardless of suit. Only the hand type ranking decides first, then card rank as a tiebreaker.

**Is A-2-3 a valid sequence?**
Yes. Ace can rank low at the bottom of a sequence, but it ranks highest in a Trail.

**What breaks a tie between two Trails?**
Compare the rank of the three cards. The higher rank wins, so 9-9-9 beats 7-7-7.
