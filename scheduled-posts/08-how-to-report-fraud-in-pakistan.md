## Direct answer

If you suspect online fraud in Pakistan, there are two separate tracks worth pursuing: report the crime itself to the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) — the agency that took over this function from the FIA's former Cyber Crime Wing / NR3C in 2024 — and separately pursue the money through your bank or wallet's own dispute process, since a criminal report and a financial recovery are not the same thing.

## Two different problems, two different paths

It's worth separating these clearly, because people often only pursue one:

1. **Reporting the crime** — filing with the relevant cybercrime authority creates an official record and can support an investigation, but it doesn't directly return your money.
2. **Recovering the money** — this runs through whichever payment method moved the funds (bank, Easypaisa, JazzCash) and their own dispute or chargeback process, which is a separate track from the criminal report.

Pursuing both, rather than just one, gives you the best realistic chance at both outcomes.

## Reporting the crime

The agency responsible for cybercrime complaints in Pakistan changed in 2024: the FIA's Cyber Crime Wing and the National Response Centre for Cyber Crime (NR3C) were merged into the **National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA)**. We're not going to publish a specific phone number or web form link here, because we found genuinely conflicting figures across different sources while researching this piece, and this site doesn't publish a number we can't personally confirm is current. Search for "NCCIA" (or, if that doesn't turn up a live official page by the time you're reading this, the FIA's own site) to find their current official contact details directly, rather than trusting a number from an older blog post — ours included, if enough time has passed.

## Recovering the money

- **If it moved through a bank:** raise a dispute with your bank directly first. If unresolved, Pakistan's **Banking Mohtasib** (banking ombudsman) handles unresolved customer complaints against banks.
- **If it moved through Easypaisa or JazzCash:** both wallets have their own in-app dispute or support process — see our [customer service](/customer-service/) page for the pattern, and go through the wallet's own official channel rather than a number someone messaged you.
- **Act quickly.** The longer a disputed transaction sits unreported, the harder it generally gets to reverse or trace.

## Evidence to gather before you report either way

- Transaction IDs, timestamps, and amounts for every payment involved.
- Screenshots of the app, website, or chat where the fraud occurred — including URLs and usernames, which can disappear or change later.
- Any communication (SMS, email, chat) with the person or platform involved.
- The exact domain or app package name, if relevant — see our [official domains guidance](/official-domains/) for how we check this ourselves.

## If it involves a fake app or copycat site

If you believe a specific app or website is impersonating a legitimate business, report it both to the platform being impersonated (if you can reach them) and to us if it concerns Pak Rummy specifically — see our [complaints page](/complaints/) for how.

## FAQ

**Is a civil lawsuit worth pursuing for a small amount?**
Usually not on its own — legal costs and time typically outweigh a small loss. The criminal report and the bank/wallet dispute process are the more proportionate first steps for most cases.

**What if the fraud happened through a foreign website?**
Still worth reporting to NCCIA, since cross-border cybercrime is explicitly part of their remit, but recovery becomes significantly harder when a foreign platform or bank is involved — report promptly rather than waiting.

**Can PakRummyOfficial.com investigate a fraud report for me?**
No — we have no investigative authority or access to any platform's systems. We can take a report about a suspected fake site impersonating Pak Rummy specifically (see [complaints](/complaints/)), but a general fraud report needs to go to the actual authorities and your bank/wallet.
