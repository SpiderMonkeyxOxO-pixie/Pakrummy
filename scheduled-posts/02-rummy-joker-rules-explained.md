## Direct answer

13-card Rummy uses two kinds of jokers — a fixed number of **printed jokers** included in the deck, and a **wild joker** picked at random for that round (any card of that rank, in any suit). Either kind can substitute for a missing card in a set or an impure sequence, but neither can be used inside a **pure sequence**, which by definition contains no joker at all.

## Printed joker vs. wild joker

- **Printed joker** — a card physically marked as a joker in the deck. It's a joker in every round, with no dependency on what's turned up.
- **Wild joker** — determined fresh each round, usually by turning up a random card from the closed deck. Every card of that same rank, regardless of suit, becomes a wild joker for that round only. A card that was wild last round is just an ordinary card this round unless the same rank comes up again.

Both function the same way once in play: either can stand in for a missing card to complete a set or a sequence that isn't required to be pure.

## What a joker can substitute for

A joker — printed or wild — can fill a missing spot in:

- **A set** (three or more cards of the same rank, different suits) — for example, 7♠-7♥-[joker] stands in as a set of three 7s.
- **An impure sequence** (three or more consecutive cards of the same suit, with at least one joker used) — for example, 4♦-5♦-[joker] stands in for 4♦-5♦-6♦.

## What a joker can never substitute for

A joker cannot be used inside a **pure sequence**. A pure sequence is defined specifically as three or more consecutive cards of the same suit with *no joker used at all* — using one, even to fill an otherwise-obvious gap, turns that group into an impure sequence instead. Since every valid declaration needs at least one pure sequence (see our [13-card Rummy walkthrough](/blog/13-card-rummy-rules-beginners-walkthrough/)), at least one of your groups must be built entirely from real, consecutive, same-suit cards.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating an impure sequence as if it satisfies the pure-sequence requirement.** It doesn't — a hand with one pure sequence and three impure groups is valid overall, but a hand with zero pure sequences cannot be declared no matter how complete it looks.
- **Forgetting the wild joker resets every round.** A rank that was wild last round carries no special status this round unless it's drawn again as that round's wild card.
- **Holding onto a genuine wild-rank card out of habit.** If you hold the actual card whose rank is wild for the round, you can usually still use it in its own suit and position within a sequence, the same as any ordinary card — it doesn't have to be used as a joker substitute.
- **Miscounting deadwood when a hand doesn't declare.** Jokers (printed or wild, if left unused in your hand) score 0 points as deadwood — see our [full scoring breakdown](/guides/rummy-scoring-explained/) for how every other card is valued.

## FAQ

**Can I use two jokers in the same group?**
Yes — a set or impure sequence can use more than one joker to fill multiple missing spots, as long as the group still has at least three cards total.

**What happens if I don't use my jokers at all?**
Unused jokers left in your hand when an opponent declares count as deadwood worth 0 points — better than a face card or number card, but still cards you'd rather have placed in a group.

**Is the wild joker the same every game?**
No — it's typically re-drawn at the start of each round, so a card that was wild in one hand usually isn't wild in the next.
