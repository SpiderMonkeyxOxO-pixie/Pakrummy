## Direct answer

Return to Player (RTP) is a theoretical long-run average — the percentage of all money wagered on a specific slot title that it's designed to pay back across a very large number of spins, not a prediction of what will happen in your next spin or your next session. A slot with a 96% RTP can still lose a player money all day, every day, and that's entirely consistent with its RTP figure.

## What RTP actually measures

RTP is calculated by the game's designer over millions of simulated or logged spins, not observed from any one player's experience. If a title has a 96% RTP, that means across a very large sample, about 96% of total wagers are paid back as winnings — with the remaining 4% being the house edge. It says nothing about:

- Whether you personally are up or down after any number of spins.
- When within those millions of spins the payouts land — they could be smoothly distributed or concentrated in rare, large wins.
- Anything about a different title, even one that looks similar.

## Why "due for a win" isn't how this works

Each spin on a properly functioning slot machine is independent — the random number generator has no memory of previous spins. A machine that's paid out less than its RTP average recently is not "due" to pay out more to compensate; the next spin's odds are exactly the same as they've always been. This is the same gambler's-fallacy pattern covered in our piece on [how crash games work](/blog/how-crash-games-work/) — different mechanic, identical misconception.

## Volatility matters as much as RTP

Two titles can share the same RTP and feel completely different to play, because of volatility (sometimes called variance):

- **Low volatility** — frequent, smaller wins, with less dramatic swings.
- **High volatility** — longer stretches with no win, offset by the possibility of a much larger one.

RTP tells you the long-run average payback; volatility tells you how bumpy the ride to get there typically is. Both numbers matter, and neither tells you what your next spin will do.

## Where to actually find a title's RTP

A specific slot title's genuine RTP, if disclosed, is usually shown in that title's own in-app information or paytable screen — not in a third-party blog post, a forum claim, or a "leaked" figure. ## FAQ

**Does a higher RTP mean I'll definitely win more?**
No — it means the theoretical long-run average favors you slightly more than a lower-RTP title, over a sample size far larger than any realistic session. Short-term results can differ enormously from the long-run average in either direction.

**Can an app's RTP change?**
In principle, yes, if the operator adjusts the title's configuration — which is exactly why a number you saw months ago, or on a different app, shouldn't be assumed to still apply.

**Is a slot "hot" or "cold" based on recent results?**
No — this is the same independent-outcome principle covered above. Recent spins on a properly functioning RNG-based machine have no bearing on the next one.
