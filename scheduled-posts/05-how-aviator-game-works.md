## Direct answer

The Aviator game is a multiplier-based betting game: a plane takes off and a multiplier rises in real time, and players must cash out before the round ends or lose their stake. A 100 PKR bet cashed out at 2.0x returns 200 PKR; waiting too long forfeits it entirely.

## How a round works, step by step

Each round begins once you've placed a bet. A plane appears on screen and climbs, with a multiplier rising alongside it — starting at 1.00x and increasing continuously. At an unpredictable moment, the round ends ("crashes"). If you've cashed out before that point, you keep your stake multiplied by whatever the multiplier showed at the moment you cashed out. If you haven't cashed out when the round ends, you lose the full stake for that round.

## A worked example

Say you bet 100 PKR. If you cash out when the multiplier reaches 2.0x, you receive 200 PKR — your original stake plus 100 PKR in winnings. If you instead wait, hoping for a higher multiplier, and the round ends at 1.8x before you've clicked cash out, you lose the full 100 PKR stake. The multiplier only pays out the moment you actively cash out — it isn't credited automatically at any point.

## Why the crash point can't be predicted

Each round's end point is generated independently, typically using an algorithmic method designed so the outcome isn't knowable in advance and can't be altered mid-round. There is no pattern across rounds — a string of early crashes doesn't make a late crash "due," and a string of high multipliers doesn't make a crash "due" either. Content claiming to predict crash points, sell "signals," or guarantee timing is not describing how these games actually work.

## FAQ

**Can you predict when it will crash?**
No. Each round's crash point is independent of previous rounds and can't be reliably predicted — treat any claim otherwise as false.

**What happens if I don't cash out in time?**
The round ends and the stake for that round is lost.

**Is Aviator the same as other crash games?**
Aviator is one specific branded title within the broader "crash game" genre — the underlying mechanic (rising multiplier, cash out before it ends) is shared across similar games.
