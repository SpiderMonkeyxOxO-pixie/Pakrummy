## Direct answer

Common Teen Patti variations include Muflis, where the lowest-ranking hand wins instead of the highest, and AK-47, where specific cards are designated wild. These variants change hand-building strategy but use the same basic betting structure as standard Teen Patti.

## Why variations exist

Standard Teen Patti rankings and betting are widely known, so many tables introduce variants to change the strategy without changing the core flow of dealing and betting. Most variants either invert the hand rankings, add wild cards, or change how many cards are in play — the blind/seen betting structure and turn order generally stay the same across all of them.

## Muflis (lowest hand wins)

In Muflis, the hand rankings from our [Teen Patti sequence guide](/blog/teen-patti-sequence-explained/) are flipped entirely — the worst hand by standard rules becomes the best hand in Muflis, and a Trail becomes the weakest possible hand instead of the strongest. This changes which cards you want to hold and discard from the ground up.

## AK-47

In this variant, specific cards — commonly Aces, Kings, 4s, and 7s — are designated wild cards that can substitute for any other card when forming a hand. Wild cards significantly change the odds of forming strong hands, since more combinations become possible.

## Other common variants

- **999**: cards close to a value of 999 (treating face cards and Aces by assigned point values) form the strongest hands, rather than standard sequences and sets.
- **Low Wild**: the lowest card dealt to any player, and all cards of that rank, become wild for that hand.
- **Joker variants**: one or more printed jokers are added to the deck and act as wild cards wherever they're dealt.

Rule details for each of these can differ by table and operator, so treat these descriptions as the general concept, not a fixed universal standard.
