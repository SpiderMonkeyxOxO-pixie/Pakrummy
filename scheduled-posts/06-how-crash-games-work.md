## Direct answer

A crash game is a betting format where a multiplier rises from 1.00x until an unpredictable "crash point," and players must cash out before then to win; waiting too long loses the full stake. Each round is mathematically independent of the last.

## How the multiplier rises

A round starts the moment bets are placed. From there, a multiplier climbs continuously — 1.01x, 1.50x, 2.00x, sometimes far higher — for as long as the round stays active. Players watch it rise and decide, in real time, when to cash out and lock in that multiplier against their stake.

## What "crash point" means, and why every round is independent

The crash point is simply the moment a given round ends. It's typically generated using a method designed to be unknowable in advance and tamper-proof once the round starts. Because each round's crash point is generated fresh, there's no real sense in which a multiplier is "overdue" after a run of low crashes, or a crash is "overdue" after a run of high ones — that's a common misconception known as the gambler's fallacy, and it doesn't hold here. Content that talks about "patterns," "hot streaks," or predictive "signals" for crash games is describing something these games don't actually have.

## Crash games vs. Aviator specifically

"Crash game" is the general name for this mechanic — a rising multiplier, a cash-out decision, an unpredictable end point. Aviator is one specific, widely recognized branded title built on that same mechanic. See our [Aviator explainer](/blog/how-aviator-game-works/) for a worked example of exactly how the payout math plays out.

## Risk framing

If you choose to play, the only thing within your control is deciding your cash-out target and your budget *before* the round starts — not adjusting your plan mid-climb based on how the multiplier is trending. That's a budgeting discipline, not a strategy that changes your odds. See our [responsible gaming](/responsible-gaming/) page for support options.

## FAQ

**Is a crash game the same as Aviator?**
Aviator is one specific branded example; "crash game" is the general genre name for the underlying mechanic.

**Can the house control when it crashes?**
In a well-built implementation, the crash point is fixed algorithmically before the round starts and isn't adjusted live — we haven't independently verified this for any specific app, including Pak Rummy's.

**What's a safe way to approach cash-out targets?**
Decide your target before the round starts, not while the multiplier is climbing — that's a budgeting habit, not a strategy claim, since no approach changes the underlying odds.
