## Direct answer

Set your gaming budget *before* you open the app, not while you're playing: pick a fixed amount you can fully afford to lose, treat it the same as any other discretionary spending, and stop the moment it's gone — regardless of how the session is going.

## Why "before you play" is the whole point

Almost everyone already knows the general idea of setting a budget. Where it actually breaks down is timing: deciding a number mid-session, after a loss, or after a win, is a different decision than deciding it cold, with no result yet pulling at your judgment. A budget set in the moment tends to expand to match whatever you've already lost — a budget set in advance doesn't move.

## A simple method

1. **Pick a fixed amount you can afford to lose completely** — not money earmarked for bills, savings, or anything else. If losing it would change your month, it's too much.
2. **Decide it in writing, somewhere you'll actually see it** — a note on your phone, not just a thought you had once.
3. **Separate that amount from the rest of your money** before you start, if your payment method allows it, so there's a physical limit rather than just a mental one.
4. **Set a session time limit alongside the money limit.** A time cap catches a different failure mode than a money cap — sessions that run long even when the stakes are small.
5. **Stop at the limit, every time, win or lose.** The rule only works if it has no exceptions. "Just this once" is how a fixed budget becomes a flexible one.

## What to do when you hit the limit

Close the app. Not "one more hand" — close it. If you notice yourself negotiating with the limit you set an hour ago, that's the clearest sign the limit was doing its job and needed to hold.

## If you're topping up mid-session

Needing to add more money mid-session, after the original budget is gone, is the single biggest warning sign that a budget isn't being followed rather than one that didn't exist. See the warning signs listed on our [responsible gaming page](/responsible-gaming/) — this specific pattern (chasing a loss with a fresh deposit) is called out there directly.

## Tools that can help, if available

If the app you're using offers deposit limits, cool-off periods, or self-exclusion inside account settings, set them proactively rather than relying on willpower alone — a system-enforced limit doesn't require you to talk yourself out of continuing. ## FAQ

**How much should I budget?**
There's no universal number — the right amount is whatever you could lose entirely without it affecting your bills, savings, or obligations. If you're unsure, start smaller than feels necessary; you can always raise it later, but you can't easily undo a loss that was too large.

**What if I go over my budget once?**
Treat it as information, not a one-time slip to ignore. Going over usually means either the limit was set too casually or it wasn't enforced with a hard stop — fix whichever one was true before your next session.

**Is a time limit really necessary if I'm tracking money?**
Yes — time and money are separate failure modes. A long session at small stakes can still cost you sleep, attention, and the next day, even if the money limit technically held.

## What this page does not cover

This is a general budgeting method, not financial or clinical advice, and it isn't specific to any single app's tools or limits.
