## Direct answer

Poker is a card game where players bet on who holds the best five-card hand, from Royal Flush (highest) down to High Card (lowest). Players can check, bet, call, raise, or fold each betting round, with the best hand — or the last player remaining — winning the pot.

## Hand rankings, highest to lowest

| Rank | Hand | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Royal Flush** | 10-J-Q-K-A, same suit |
| 2 | **Straight Flush** | 5-6-7-8-9, same suit |
| 3 | **Four of a Kind** | 9-9-9-9-2 |
| 4 | **Full House** | K-K-K-4-4 |
| 5 | **Flush** | 2-5-8-J-K, same suit, not in order |
| 6 | **Straight** | 4-5-6-7-8, mixed suits |
| 7 | **Three of a Kind** | 7-7-7-2-9 |
| 8 | **Two Pair** | J-J-4-4-8 |
| 9 | **One Pair** | 10-10-3-6-K |
| 10 | **High Card** | No pair or sequence — highest card wins |

When two hands are the same type, the one with the higher-ranking cards wins the comparison.

## Basic betting actions

On your turn, you can:

- **Check** — pass the action without betting, only possible if no one has bet yet this round.
- **Bet** — put chips into the pot, which others must match to stay in.
- **Call** — match the current bet to continue.
- **Raise** — increase the bet, forcing others to match the higher amount or fold.
- **Fold** — give up your hand and whatever you've already bet, exiting the round.

## A simple round, step by step

Without assuming any one specific variant, a typical round runs: cards are dealt, a betting round follows, more cards may be revealed depending on the variant, another betting round follows, and remaining players reach a **showdown** — revealing their hands, with the best hand taking the pot. If only one player remains after everyone else folds, that player wins without needing to show their cards at all.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Playing too many hands.** Folding early and often is normal, even expected — it's not a sign you're playing badly.
- **Ignoring betting patterns.** How much and when opponents bet tells you as much as your own cards do.
- **Chasing losses.** Increasing your bets to recover an earlier loss raises your risk without improving your actual hand.

## FAQ

**What beats what in poker?**
Royal Flush is highest, then Straight Flush, Four of a Kind, Full House, Flush, Straight, Three of a Kind, Two Pair, One Pair, and High Card lowest.

**What's a showdown?**
When betting ends with two or more players still in the hand, remaining hands are revealed and compared — the best hand wins the pot.

**Do I need to know every variant to start?**
No. Hand rankings and the basic betting actions above are shared across nearly every poker variant; variant-specific structure (like blinds or community cards) can come later.
